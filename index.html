I'm a Stanford student studying tech and policy. I love building and scaling projects, from founding an NGO reaching 10k+ students to researching growth strategies for fintech startups. I'm looking towards opportunities in strategy and business, with a side obsession with AI policy.

My eighth-grade detention slip read: “asks too many questions.”

I’ve since turned that habit into work: building a 30-country model to figure out where foreign aid does the most good, pitching a growth strategy to a startup’s CEO and board, and managing political campaign ads during a pivotal election.

Closer to home, I founded FirstGen Mentors to answer the career questions immigrant students like me kept running into, and learned that plans only scale when they're built around the people using them.

That's the part of strategy I love most: finding where a plan breaks against reality and fixing it. I do that now as a strategist at Rem and Company.

Next, I want to work on companies building with AI, especially startups, where the rules are still being written. Every company is about to make decisions that are part product and part policy: what to automate, what to disclose, who's accountable when a model gets it wrong. I want to be the person in the room who understands both sides, helping teams move fast without losing the trust they depend on.

Outside of work, I'm usually on a pickleball court, catching up on Formula 1, planning my next trip, finding a new place to eat, or psychoanalyzing a Nolan movie.
